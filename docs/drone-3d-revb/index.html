Fly-Brain Drone Rev B

The drone as it stands now. The motors moved off the PCB into a one-piece printed frame, and the PCB shrank to a 46 × 44 mm board in the middle. The frame is the real printed mesh and the board is textured from the real copper, mask and drill files. Every other part is modelled at its real size and height, wired the way it will be soldered.

    What changed

    Three versions so far. The parts, the wiring and the ESP32 pins are the same in all of them; what moved is how everything is held together.

    Version 1 · perfboard

    Printed frame, perfboard driver

    MOSFETs, diodes and resistors hand-wired on a 4 × 6 cm perfboard, everything else on a printed frame. Old 3D model

    Rev A · PCB as the frame

    83 × 83 mm X-shaped PCB

    The board itself was the frame: motors in 8.8 mm holes at the arm tips, MOSFETs lying flat, battery in a tray screwed under it. Never ordered; Ayham found it too big.

    Rev B · now

    46 × 44 mm board in a printed frame

    A small flight-controller board on four posts in a 5.6 g printed X. Designed and checked, not ordered or printed yet.

      Props stay off for every motor test. Charge the LiPo at 500 mA or less, unplugged from the drone. Measure 3.3 V on the front 3V3 pad before the ESP32 goes on.

      Pads on the board

      The silkscreen has no text yet; Ayham is adding labels in KiCad. Front row, left to right: G4, G3, 3V3, SCL, SDA, GND, INT, G2, G1. Rear row: BAT+ (square), BAT−, OUT, GND, IN, then C1. The ESP32's pinout is still unconfirmed, so every signal goes through a short wire instead of straight into it.

      PadGoes toWireWhat it is

      Still open

      1. Measure the real parts against the board before ordering: TPS63020 module 20 × 12 mm or smaller, GY-521 pin order, MOSFET legs 1.1 mm or thinner.
      2. Photo of the ESP32 board's pin labels and a ruler, to confirm the pins and its size. The ESP32 layout in this model is approximate.
      3. Test-print one motor tube and adjust the fit (TUBE_ID, now 8.6 mm).
      4. A camera mount. The OV2640 hangs on its ribbon at the front for now.
      5. Weigh everything and measure one motor's thrust. With about 70 g to lift, each motor needs about 35 g of thrust for a 2:1 margin.
      6. Which motor order and spin directions ESP-Drone expects, compared with M1 front-right CCW, M2 rear-right CW, M3 rear-left CCW, M4 front-left CW.
